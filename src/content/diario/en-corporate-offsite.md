If you are choosing where to take a team, the question that matters is not whether the place is beautiful. It is whether it can carry your programme, whether the travel pays for itself, and whether you can contract it without it becoming a problem for your finance team.

This page answers all three — including the parts that may take us off your list.

## What the place is

Thirteen bungalows on the bank of the Rio Arapiuns, in Pará, inside the Tapajós-Arapiuns Extractive Reserve — an hour and a half upriver by boat from Alter do Chão. Up to twenty-six people. Raised timber, palm-thatched roofs, solar power, and a team who live here.

On exclusive use, nobody outside your team is on site on those dates. That is the difference between an offsite and an incentive trip sharing breakfast with strangers.

## Where the programme happens

**The pavilion** is the plenary room: a long palm-thatched structure, open on all four sides, that seats the whole team together. It is where the long session, the presentation and the strategy discussion fit.

**The shala** is the second space, enclosed enough for a smaller group to break away.

**The dining hall** — everyone at the same table, three times a day, for several days running. For a team that is as much a working space as the pavilion. It is where the conversations that do not fit the agenda happen.

**The beach and the hammock grove** are where people manage not to be in a session. An offsite without those two becomes a long meeting in an expensive place.

## Disconnection is the product, and also the risk

Worth being blunt here, because this is where most corporate decisions stall.

Connectivity is deliberately weak. There is Starlink, kept for emergencies. **It does not sustain a whole team working online.** If your programme requires people to keep answering their day job, this is not the place — and we would rather lose the booking than host a frustrated team.

If the point is the opposite — pulling a team out of the flow for a few days in a way nobody can circumvent — the geography does that work by itself. It is not a phones-in-a-box policy. It is the river.

## The content the place already has

The part of the programme we do not have to invent: canoeing the flooded forest, the Samaúma trail out of the Atodi community, the cassava-flour process, palm weaving with the artisans, night dive-fishing taught by local fishermen.

These are led by the families who live from them and paid directly to them — so the team needs to bring cash in reais. This is not a team-building exercise designed by a consultancy: it is real work, done by the people who do it, with the team learning alongside. For most groups it produces more conversation than any designed exercise.

The **piracaia** — fish baked in the sand, in banana leaf, by candlelight, served in a circle on the beach — tends to close the last night without anyone having to design a closing ceremony.

## Getting here

Fly to Santarém (STM), direct from Manaus, Belém and Brasília, connecting from São Paulo. Then 40 minutes by road and 90 minutes by boat.

Count a full travel day at each end. We arrange the boat as part of the group package; flights are on you.

## What is not here

No spa, no gym, no enclosed meeting room with conference AV. There is a pavilion, a dining hall, a beach and a forest.

No beaches from February to June — the river rises and the sand goes under. It is a beautiful, different season, but do not sell your team a beach in April.

And we are not a plant-medicine venue. Said plainly, because the Amazon invites the assumption.

## How we quote

The nightly rate depends on group size and number of nights, and falls as the group grows. We quote per group rather than publishing a fixed table.

Send the dates, how many people, how many nights, and what your programme actually needs from a space. You get a real number back.
